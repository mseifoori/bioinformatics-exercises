## Insight 1:
- Origin: When learning **Alignment, PAM & BLOSUM Matrices**

- Concept:
    > Suppose a bacterial strain is associated with increased disease severity, and a major virulence factor produced by a plasmid-encoded gene shows sequence differences compared with the previously known form of the protein.
    >
    > A possible approach would be to use sequence alignment algorithms to identify and characterize the amino-acid changes between the known and newly observed protein sequences. PAM/BLOSUM substitution matrices could then provide information about the evolutionary plausibility of these substitutions, while Multiple Sequence Alignment and conservation analysis could help determine whether the affected positions are evolutionarily conserved.
    >
    > These findings could be combined with functional-domain and structural analyses to investigate whether the observed mutations may affect the protein's structure or function, potentially contributing to the increased virulence phenotype.
    >
    > Such an analysis could provide useful insights for further experimental investigation and, ultimately, for understanding the virulence mechanism and identifying potential targets for therapeutic or anti-virulence strategies.
